1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & COMPARABILITY VERDICT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUESTION: Does the current depth-experiment data permit a fair direct comparison?
VERDICT: NO. The methods cannot be directly compared on a single unconditioned curve.

RATIONALE:
A rigorous audit reveals that each experiment used distinct protocols:
  1. Measurement Timing:
     - Baseline Depth: Measured at INITIALIZATION (0 training steps).
     - Layer-Wise Corrected & Fixed Combined: Measured AFTER 2 gradient descent steps.
     - Dynamic Adaptive Trigger: Measured BEFORE the 2 training steps.
  2. Parameter Scope:
     - Baseline: Measures parameter 0 of LAYER 0 (followed by L-1 scrambling layers).
     - Layer-Wise, Combined, & Trigger: Measure parameter 0 of the ACTIVE LAYER l (at end).
  3. Freezing & Training Scope:
     - Baseline: Entire circuit initialized at once; no parameters trained or frozen.
     - Layer-Wise & Combined: Earlier layers are permanently frozen from depth 1.
     - Adaptive Trigger: All layers trained simultaneously before trigger; earlier layers
       frozen only after trigger activates.
  4. Sample Size Asymmetry:
     - Baseline Depth uses N = 250 trials.
     - Mitigations use N = 100 trials.

2. ADAPTIVE TRIGGER STATISTIC CLARIFICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The adaptive trigger script (adaptive_trigger.py) evaluates a per-trial trigger condition:
  v_curr < 0.05 * v_ref
where:
  - v_curr is the variance across all 18 gradient components of the active layer within trial k.
  - v_ref is the intra-layer variance of layer 1 within trial k.

However, the output CSV (results_adaptive_trigger.csv) reports:
  - current_variance: Population variance across 100 trials of parameter 0 (0.30819 at depth 1).
  - depth_1_reference: Mean of intra-layer variances across 100 trials (0.028605).
  - trigger_threshold: 0.05 * depth_1_reference (0.001430).

Therefore, 'current_variance' is NOT the statistic tested against 'trigger_threshold'.
They represent two completely different variance metrics.

4. VALID SCIENTIFIC CONCLUSIONS SUPPORTED BY DATA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Baseline barren plateau verified: As depth increases from L=1 to L=8, gradient variance
   of layer 0 collapses from 0.2737 to 0.0099, saturating at ~0.0075 for L >= 8.
2. Layer-wise training alone fails in deep circuits: When adding uniformly random layers,
   gradient variance of the active layer drops from 0.1523 at L=1 to 0.0076 at L=20,
   converging to the unmitigated baseline floor.
3. Fixed combination prevents collapse: Appending Normal(0, 0.1) initialized layers keeps
   post-training active layer variance above 0.0195 at all depths (0.0257 at L=20).
4. Dynamic adaptive trigger activates as depth increases: Trigger rate rises from 0% at L=1
   to 62% at L=20 (mean trigger depth = 9.98 layers). Active layer gradient variance at L=20
   is recorded at 0.0535.

5. REMAINING LIMITATIONS & FUTURE REQUIREMENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- To achieve a true apples-to-apples comparison, an experiment must be run where all methods
  record BOTH pre-training and post-training gradients on the exact same parameter indices.
- Higher gradient variance does not guarantee convergence to the global optimum.
- Finite sampling noise (shot noise) has not been evaluated.
