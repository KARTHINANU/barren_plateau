1. TRACE OF ADAPTIVE TRIGGER IMPLEMENTATION & DECISION STATISTIC
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In `adaptive_trigger.py` (lines 68-96):
1. When layer l is added, all 18 parameter-shift gradients are computed for the new layer:
     g_active = [∂C/∂θ_{l,0}, ∂C/∂θ_{l,1}, ..., ∂C/∂θ_{l,17}]
2. The WITHIN-TRIAL active-layer sample variance is calculated:
     v_curr = np.var(g_active) = (1/18) * sum_{j=0}^{17} (g_{l,j} - mean(g_active))^2
3. At layer 1, this variance establishes that trial's individual reference:
     v_ref = v_curr(layer=1)
4. For layer l > 1, the trigger condition is evaluated:
     IF v_curr < 0.05 * v_ref:
         mitigation_active = True
         reinitialize active layer ~ Normal(0, 0.1)
         recalculate g_active with Normal initialization
         v_curr = np.var(g_active)
         permanently lock mitigation_active = True for remaining layers

2. WHY THE TRIGGER STATISTIC AND REPORTED CSV VARIANCE ARE DIFFERENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A critical finding of this audit is that `results_adaptive_trigger.csv` presents
two completely different statistical quantities side by side:

  Statistic A: Within-Trial Intra-Layer Variance (Trigger Statistic)
    - Evaluated across 18 DIFFERENT parameter gradients within a single trial k.
    - Mean value at Layer 1 across 100 trials: v_ref_mean = 0.028605.
    - Trigger Threshold: 0.05 * 0.028605 = 0.001430.

  Statistic B: Cross-Trial Population Variance (Reported Current Variance)
    - Evaluated for ONE SINGLE parameter (θ_0, Rx gate on qubit 0) across 100 trials.
    - Reported in CSV as `current_variance` = 0.308193 at depth 1.

EXPLANATION OF APPARENT PARADOX:
In the CSV, `current_variance` (0.3082) is > 200x larger than `trigger_threshold` (0.00143).
This is NOT an error. Across random trials with Uniform(0, 2pi) initialization, the first
layer's gradient g_0 has a broad distribution spanning roughly [-0.8, +0.8], giving a cross-trial
variance of ~0.308. However, within any single trial, the 18 gates produce expectation values
centered close to each other, giving a within-trial variance of ~0.028.
The trigger decision operates SOLELY on Statistic A, whereas the final comparison table
evaluates Statistic B.

5. STATISTICAL UNCERTAINTY & SAMPLE SIZE LIMITATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Original Experiment (N = 100 trials):
  - Final activation rate at Depth 20: p = 62/100 (62.0%).
  - Standard Error of proportion: SE = sqrt(p * (1-p) / N) = sqrt(0.62 * 0.38 / 100) = 4.85%.
  - 95% Confidence Interval (Normal approx): [52.5%, 71.5%].
  - Interpretation: With 95% confidence, the true activation rate of this circuit under the
    5% threshold lies between 52.5% and 71.5%.

Sample Size Limitations:
  - N = 100 is sufficient to observe the transition from 0% to ~62% activation.
  - However, resolving fine differences in gradient variance requires large budgets due to
    inherent circuit-to-circuit variance.
  - In statevector simulation, sample variance has an estimator standard error of:
      SE(Var) ≈ Var * sqrt(2 / (N - 1)) ≈ 0.142 * Var (approx 14% relative error for N=100).

6. SCIENTIFIC GUARDRAILS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Gradient variance preservation does NOT prove successful optimization:
   - Maintaining Var(g) ~ 0.0535 at depth 20 indicates that cost-function flatness is avoided.
   - It does NOT prove that gradient descent converges to the global minimum of any physical problem.
2. The 5% threshold is an empirical heuristic:
   - It was chosen based on the depth transition region where baseline gradients drop to 5%.
   - It is not a universal mathematical constant derived from representation theory.
