Physical Interpretation:
1. Group 1 (Baseline) collapses across all 4 metrics to the plateau floor (~0.006 - 0.007).
2. Group 2 (Controlled Init Only) maintains healthy variance (> 0.016) across the ENTIRE
   circuit without needing layer-wise freezing. This isolates the power of identity init.
3. Group 3 (Layer-Wise Only, Uniform) completely collapses to baseline levels (~0.0073)
   at depth 20, proving that layer-wise training alone fails against scrambling.
4. Group 4 (Fixed Combined) preserves strong variance across both early and late gates.
5. Group 5 (Adaptive Trigger) achieves the highest active-layer variance at initialization
   (0.04278, 5.6x higher than baseline) by rescuing collapsing trials. However, because early
   layers were initialized uniformly before triggering, Layer 0 gradients remain small (~0.007).

4. CORRECTIONS & LANGUAGE NARROWING NEEDED FOR FINAL DOCUMENTATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To maintain rigorous scientific accuracy, the final report must adhere to the following
framing rules:

1. DO NOT CLAIM MATHEMATICAL PROOF OF ASYMPTOTIC BARREN PLATEAUS:
   - State: "Our empirical simulations over n = 2..7 qubits and depths L = 1..20 exhibit
     exponential gradient variance decay consistent with McClean et al. (2018)."
   - Do not claim our finite statevector experiments mathematically prove asymptotic theorems.

2. DO NOT CLAIM UNITARY 2-DESIGN FORMATION:
   - State: "The variance saturates to a floor for L >= 8, characteristic of circuits
     approximating unitary 2-design properties."
   - Do not assert that the circuit strictly forms a Haar 2-design without Frame potential tests.

3. DO NOT EQUATE GRADIENT VARIANCE TO OPTIMIZATION SUCCESS:
   - State: "Preserving gradient variance avoids cost landscape flatness (trainability),
     providing non-vanishing descent directions."
   - Do not claim that higher variance guarantees convergence to the global ground state
     of an arbitrary Hamiltonian. Near-identity initialization may restrict initial search space.

4. NARROW CLAIMS ON THE ADAPTIVE TRIGGER:
   - State: "The adaptive trigger successfully rescues active-layer trainability (0.0428 at T0)
     while preserving unconstrained exploration during early layers."
   - Acknowledge that the first-layer gradient in Group 5 remains small (~0.0069) because
     early layers were trained and frozen under uniform initialization before triggering.
