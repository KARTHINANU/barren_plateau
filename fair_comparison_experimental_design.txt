1. SCIENTIFIC OBJECTIVE & RATIONALE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Previous audit phases identified that existing experiments could not be directly
compared on a single curve due to four methodological confounders:
  1. Timing Confounder: Baseline was measured at step 0 (init); layer-wise and
     fixed combined were measured at step 2 (post-training); adaptive trigger
     was measured at step 0 (pre-training).
  2. Parameter Target Confounder: Baseline measured Layer 0 (followed by L-1
     scrambling layers); mitigations measured Active Layer l (at circuit end).
  3. Freezing/Training Confounder: Baseline trained no parameters; layer-wise
     froze prior layers; adaptive trigger trained all layers before triggering.
  4. Metric Confounder: Adaptive trigger decision rule used intra-layer variance
     across 18 parameters, while CSV tables reported cross-trial variance of 1 parameter.

The objective of this unified experimental design is to establish a rigorous,
apples-to-apples protocol where all experimental groups are evaluated under
identical conditions, isolating the causal contributions of:
  (a) Near-identity initialization,
  (b) Sequential layer-wise growth and parameter freezing,
  (c) The dynamic adaptive variance-trigger decision rule.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. CONTROLLED CONSTANTS ACROSS ALL GROUPS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The following parameters and simulation settings are held strictly constant across
all experimental groups:

  - System Size: Fixed n = 6 qubits.
  - Circuit Depths Evaluated: L ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20}.
  - Circuit Architecture:
      * Single-qubit rotations: Rx(θ) -> Ry(θ) -> Rz(θ) on all qubits (18 params/layer).
      * Entanglement Topology: Linear nearest-neighbor CNOT chain (q -> q+1).
  - Observable & Cost Function:
      * Local Pauli-Z on qubit 0: O = Z_0 ⊗ I_5 (SparsePauliOp("IIIIIZ")).
      * Cost: C(θ) = Re <ψ(θ)| O |ψ(θ)> ∈ [-1, +1].
  - Simulator:
      * Qiskit 1.3.2 exact Statevector algebra (zero finite-sampling shot noise).
  - Gradient Evaluation:
      * Exact analytical parameter-shift rule: g_j = [C(θ + π/2) - C(θ - π/2)] / 2.
  - Training Budget:
      * Exactly 2 gradient descent steps at learning rate η = 0.5 per active layer.
  - Trial Count:
      * N = 100 independent random circuit trials per group.
  - Random Seed Policy:
      * Documented, reproducible seed schedule: base seed = 42, with synchronized
        per-trial seeds (seed = 42 + trial_id) ensuring identical starting parameters
        across groups where applicable.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. STANDARDIZED MEASUREMENT PROTOCOL (RESOLVING PAST CONFOUNDERS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To resolve the timing and parameter target confounders, every group will measure
and log FOUR standardized gradient metrics at each evaluated depth:

  Timing Points (Logged Separately):
    - Timing T_0 (Initialization): Measured immediately after layer parameters are
      instantiated, BEFORE any gradient descent steps are applied.
    - Timing T_2 (Post-Training): Measured immediately AFTER the 2 gradient descent
      steps are applied to the trainable parameters.

  Parameter Targets (Logged Separately):
    - Parameter θ_first: Parameter 0 of Layer 0 (Rx on qubit 0 of the first layer).
      * Tests whether gradient signals survive through the entire L-layer circuit
        back to the earliest gate.
    - Parameter θ_active: Parameter 0 of the Active Layer l (Rx on qubit 0 of the latest layer).
      * Tests the immediate trainability of newly added gates at the circuit output.
      * Note: At Depth 1, θ_first and θ_active are mathematically identical.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. PROPOSED EXPERIMENTAL GROUPS (ISOLATION OF VARIABLES)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To rigorously decouple the mitigation mechanisms, we propose five distinct groups:

GROUP 1: STANDARD FULL-CIRCUIT BASELINE (Control 1 — Full Circuit, Uniform)
  - Purpose: Unmitigated reference representing standard VQC execution.
  - Architecture: Full L-layer circuit instantiated at once.
  - Initialization: Uniform(0, 2π) for all 18*L parameters.
  - Freezing: No parameters frozen.
  - Training: 2 steps of full-circuit gradient descent across all 18*L parameters.
  - Measurements: θ_first and θ_active at T_0 and T_2.

GROUP 2: CONTROLLED INITIALIZATION ONLY (Control 2 — Full Circuit, Normal)
  - Purpose: Isolates near-identity initialization without layer-wise training.
  - Architecture: Full L-layer circuit instantiated at once.
  - Initialization: Normal(0, 0.1^2) for all 18*L parameters.
  - Freezing: No parameters frozen.
  - Training: 2 steps of full-circuit gradient descent across all 18*L parameters.
  - Measurements: θ_first and θ_active at T_0 and T_2.
  - Scientific Value: Tests whether near-identity initialization alone prevents
    barren plateaus in deep full-circuit architectures.

GROUP 3: LAYER-WISE TRAINING ONLY (Control 3 — Sequential Growth, Uniform)
  - Purpose: Isolates sequential layer growth and freezing without identity init.
  - Architecture: Circuit grows layer-by-layer (strictly l layers at depth l).
  - Initialization: Uniform(0, 2π) for each newly added layer.
  - Freezing: Prior layers 1..(l-1) are permanently frozen.
  - Training: 2 steps of gradient descent on active layer (18 parameters).
  - Measurements: θ_first (frozen gate) and θ_active at T_0 and T_2.
  - Scientific Value: Tests the literature claim of pure layer-wise training.

GROUP 4: FIXED COMBINED METHOD (Control 4 — Sequential Growth, Normal)
  - Purpose: Benchmark representing the static combination of both mitigations.
  - Architecture: Circuit grows layer-by-layer.
  - Initialization: Normal(0, 0.1^2) for each newly added layer.
  - Freezing: Prior layers 1..(l-1) are permanently frozen.
  - Training: 2 steps of gradient descent on active layer (18 parameters).
  - Measurements: θ_first and θ_active at T_0 and T_2.
  - Scientific Value: Establishes the performance ceiling of static combined mitigation.

GROUP 5: DYNAMIC ADAPTIVE TRIGGER (Novel Strategy — Variance-Triggered Switch)
  - Purpose: Tests the proposed data-informed dynamic switching rule.
  - Architecture: Circuit grows layer-by-layer.
  - Decision Logic:
      * Active layer initially drawn from Uniform(0, 2π).
      * Measures intra-layer variance v_curr across all 18 active-layer gradients.
      * At layer 1, records reference v_ref = v_curr.
      * For layer l > 1: if v_curr < 0.05 * v_ref, activate mitigation:
          -> Reinitialize active layer ~ Normal(0, 0.1^2).
          -> Recalculate gradients.
          -> Freeze all prior layers 1..(l-1).
          -> Permanently lock mitigation active for subsequent layers.
  - Training:
      * If unmitigated: 2 steps of full-circuit GD on all 18*l parameters.
      * If mitigated: 2 steps of GD on active layer (18 parameters) with frozen prior layers.
  - Measurements: θ_first and θ_active at T_0 and T_2.
  - Full Diagnostic Logging: Log v_curr, v_ref, threshold, trigger status, and trigger depth.

5. OUTPUT DATA STRUCTURE & STATISTICAL METRICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The unified benchmark will generate a consolidated dataset (`benchmark_fair_comparison.csv`)
with the following exact columns:

  1. group_id (1 to 5)
  2. group_name (e.g., "Full-Circuit Uniform", "Adaptive Trigger")
  3. depth (1, 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20)
  4. timing ("T0_Init" or "T2_PostTrain")
  5. parameter_scope ("theta_first_L0" or "theta_active_Ll")
  6. mean_gradient (Sample mean across N=100 trials)
  7. gradient_variance (Cross-trial population variance, ddof=0)
  8. gradient_std (Standard deviation across trials)
  9. variance_std_error (SE(Var) = Var * sqrt(2 / (N-1)))
  10. trial_count (N = 100)
  11. mitigation_active_pct (For Group 5: percentage of trials active)
  12. mean_trigger_depth (For Group 5: average depth where trigger fired)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. SCIENTIFIC GUARDRAILS & CLAIMS DISCIPLINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Trainability vs. Optimization: Preserving non-zero gradient variance confirms
   that the cost landscape avoids barren plateau concentration. It does NOT prove
   that gradient descent reaches the global minimum of any physical problem.
2. Controlled Comparison: The dynamic adaptive trigger (Group 5) will be considered
   beneficial only if it demonstrates higher gradient variance or better exploration
   trade-offs than both pure layer-wise (Group 3) and fixed combined (Group 4)
   under identical T_0/T_2 and θ_first/θ_active conditions.
3. Heuristic Nature of Threshold: The 5% threshold is an empirical heuristic,
   not an analytical invariant.
