3. PROFESSOR CONVERGENCE TOLERANCE AUDIT (epsilon = 10^4 vs 10^-4)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A key finding of this smoke test is the empirical behavior of step-by-step cost changes:
  Observed consecutive cost changes |C_t - C_{t-1}|:
  Mean step Delta: 0.032946 | Min: 0.000000 | Max: 0.537805
  1. Written criterion (epsilon = 10^4):
     Because max(|Delta C|) = 0.5378 << 10,000, setting stopping tolerance to 10^4
     causes EVERY run to terminate at Step 1 with ZERO meaningful optimization progress.
  2. Provisional criterion (epsilon = 10^-4):
     Under Adam, 6.0% of steps exhibited |Delta C| < 10^-4 near convergence.
  Conclusion: Fixed maximum iteration budget remains the only mathematically valid
  stopping criterion until the professor explicitly confirms the intended tolerance.

4. COMPARATIVE SCIENTIFIC EVALUATION & RECOMMENDATION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Adam Performance:
   - Achieves consistent, smooth cost reduction across all seeds (Mean Delta C = +0.551).
   - However, each iteration requires full parameter-shift gradient computation (2P evals).
   - At 50 iterations, Adam consumed 2,497 circuit evaluations per trial.
   - For full Phase 7 (N=50, n=6, L=12, P=216), 100 Adam steps would require ~43,200 evals/circuit,
     resulting in an estimated wall-clock time of 75+ hours.
2. COBYLA Performance:
   - Derivative-free: Evaluates solely the cost function (50 evaluations = ~50 circuit evals).
   - Achieved solid cost reduction (Mean Delta C = +0.339) using only ~50 evaluations.
   - Per-trial evaluation cost is ~50x lower than Adam.
   - For full Phase 7, COBYLA would complete in ~1.5 to 2.0 hours on CPU.
3. Final Methodological Recommendation:
   - COBYLA is the highly practical, scalable choice for the full N=50 Phase 7 benchmark on CPU.
   - Adam is recommended for small diagnostic pilot checks (n=4, low depth).
