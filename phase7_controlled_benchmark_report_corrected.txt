1. STATISTICAL CORRECTIONS APPLIED
================================================================================
The independent audit identified that the preliminary Phase 7 report utilized
standard normal critical values (z = 1.96) and population standard deviations
(ddof = 0) to calculate 95% confidence intervals, which underestimated parameter
uncertainty for a small sample size of N = 10 trials per condition.

Exact Statistical Formulations Corrected:
1. Sample Variance and Sample Standard Deviation:
   The divisor was corrected from N = 10 (ddof = 0) to N - 1 = 9 (ddof = 1):
     s = sqrt( 1 / (N - 1) * sum_{i=1}^N (x_i - bar{x})^2 ) = sigma_{pop} * sqrt(10 / 9)
   This corrects the 5.41% downward bias in dispersion.

2. Exact Standard Error of the Mean:
     SE = s / sqrt(N) = s / sqrt(10)

3. Exact Two-Sided Student's t Distribution Critical Value:
   For df = N - 1 = 9 at alpha = 0.05 (95% two-sided confidence):
     t_{0.975, 9} = 2.262157 (replacing the large-sample normal approximation z = 1.960000).
   The Student-t multiplier is 15.42% wider than the normal z-multiplier.

4. Corrected 95% Confidence Interval:
     CI_{95%} = [ bar{x} - t_{0.975, 9} * SE,  bar{x} + t_{0.975, 9} * SE ]
   Combined effect: The corrected confidence intervals are ~21.7% wider than the
   preliminary report, properly reflecting the empirical uncertainty of N = 10 trials.

3. SCIENTIFIC INTERPRETATION & METHODOLOGICAL DISTINCTIONS
================================================================================
3.1 Clarification of Cost Metrics: Initial vs Final vs Marginal vs Cumulative:
A major methodological insight from this benchmark is the necessity of separating
different optimization metrics:

1. Initial Cost C(T0) Asymmetry:
   - Haar-random uniform states yield C(T0) ~ 0.0 (maximum potential drop Delta C <= 1.0).
   - Controlled near-zero initialization forces C(T0) ~ +0.95 (maximum potential drop Delta C <= 2.0).
   - Therefore, reporting Delta C alone unfairly inflates Controlled Initialization.
   - For example, at n=6, L=12, Controlled Init achieves Delta C = +1.101, but terminates
     at a poor final energy of C(Tfin) = -0.204. Conversely, Baseline Uniform achieves
     Delta C = +0.826 and reaches a far superior final energy of C(Tfin) = -0.785.

2. Marginal vs Cumulative Layerwise Optimization:
   - In Full-Circuit methods (Baseline, Controlled Init), C(T0) is the completely untrained circuit.
   - In Layerwise methods (Layerwise Standard, Fixed Combined, Adaptive), C(T0) at depth L
     is measured at the entrance of layer L, AFTER layers 1..L-1 have already been optimized!
   - At n=6, L=12, Adaptive Enhanced enters layer 12 with C(T0) = -0.802 because prior
     layers already optimized the quantum state close to the ground state (-1.0).
   - Its marginal Delta C during layer 12 training is +0.065 (reaching C(Tfin) = -0.867).
   - Comparing marginal Delta C (+0.065) directly to full-circuit Delta C (+0.826) without
     recognizing this cumulative difference is an apples-to-oranges error. Both C(Tfin)
     and Delta C must be analyzed together.

3.2 Correction of Scientific Claims:
1. Necessary Condition Claim:
   - Original claim: "Elevating gradient variance is a NECESSARY condition for optimization convergence."
   - Corrected scientific statement: Non-vanishing gradient variance across random initializations
     is a necessary condition to prevent exponential gradient concentration (barren plateaus)
     under standard first-order gradient descent. However, general optimization convergence
     does not require global non-vanishing variance (e.g. adaptive preconditioned, second-order,
     or gradient-free optimizers).
   - Furthermore, the "Fixed Combined" paradox empirically demonstrates that high gradient variance
     (Var(g) = 0.067 at n=6, L=12) is NOT sufficient for optimization progress (Delta C = +0.030).

2. Mechanism Claim:
   - Original claim: "Freezing prior layers confines optimization to an affine subspace..."
   - Corrected scientific statement: While the affine subspace constraint is a plausible
     theoretical hypothesis explaining why Fixed Combined fails, empirical numerical simulations
     on 4- and 6-qubit toy models provide supportive empirical evidence rather than formal
     mathematical proof of a universal physical mechanism.

3. Scope and Superiority:
   - These findings represent small-scale CPU simulations on 4- and 6-qubit circuits with
     a 10-trial exploratory pilot budget per cell.
   - No method evaluated universally solves barren plateaus. Adaptive mitigation achieves
     superior selective convergence in this configuration, but scaling to large qubit counts
     remains subject to asymptotic exponential bounds.
